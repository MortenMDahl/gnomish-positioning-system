# AI Agent Handoff: Gnomish Positioning System (GPS)

GPS is a World of Warcraft addon for **WoW Forever** that replaces the cartoon world map with a zoomable top-down map built from the game's own minimap images. A 3D (perspective) mode is being prototyped separately. The design, measurements and decisions are in `INTEGRATION_PLAN.md`; read it before changing anything.

## Layout

- `Interface/AddOns/GnomishPositioningSystem/`: the addon (about 3 MB).
  - `Core.lua`: the 2D map. Standalone frame (`/gps`, key binding in `Bindings.xml`), left-drag pans, the wheel zooms around the cursor, the Me ring centres on the player and follows, `<` `>` switch maps, the `2D` ring switches to the GPS3D window at the same place and size. The look is "minimap rings" (option B of the 2026-10-16 look study, the "GPS look options" design canvas): frameless with a 1 px black edge, title and map switch at the top left, Blizzard's red close button (`RedButton-Exit` atlases), a column of round buttons in the minimap's gold ring down the right edge (`MiniMap-TrackingBorder` over `UI-Minimap-Background`, `UI-Minimap-ZoomButton-Highlight` on hover; labels and icons brass at rest, gold when active), two rings like the others with a small `+` / `-` (9 px) drawn from `WHITE8X8` bars at the bottom right (until 2026-10-17 the minimap's `ui-hud-minimap-zoom-*` atlases, whose sign was too big), and where you are at the bottom left; the mouse controls are in the rings' tooltips. Built by `ns.ApplyChrome` (`ui.SetRings(list)` lays out the column) and shared with GPS3D through the global `GPS_API` (`ApplyChrome`, `RingButton`, `TextButton`, `CopyGeometry`, `colors`, `MEDIA`, `WhereText`, `Show`). Debugging: `/gps test` (self-test report), `/gps grid` (tile numbers), `/gps where`, `/gps swapaxes` and `/gps flipfacing` (toggles if the self-test finds the position axes or facing reversed).
  - `Data/MapTiles.lua`: generated. Per map: the minimap FileDataID of every ADT tile (`tiles[y * 64 + x]`), its bounding box, the box of its main land (`fit*`), and its overview atlases.
  - `Media/*.tga`: GPS's own icons (compass needle, the camera-mode ring's three icons CamFollow, CamBehind and CamFree, the grid square of the straight-down ring), 64x64 32-bit TGA, drawn by `scripts/make_media.py`.
  - `Overview/*.jpg`: generated. Zoomed-out images, 64 px per tile, up to 16x16 tiles per atlas.
- `prototypes/GPS3D/`: the 3D view (`/gps3d`, depends on GPS, saves `GPS3D_DB`).
  - Terrain for the whole game: `Data/World_<map>.lua` (16 maps with outdoor terrain, 2,277 tiles, 4.8 MB; generated by `scripts/build_world.py`): per ADT tile a 33x33 height grid (16.7 yd apart; ground, liquid surface or sea level, whichever is highest), 2 base64 characters per height. Tiles around the camera become triangles at a level of detail chosen by distance (cell 16.7 to 533 yd) within a triangle budget (3,200 by default, `/gps3d budget`), edges matched to coarser neighbours (no cracks), tiles outside the view skipped. A tile uses its minimap image (FileDataID from GPS's MapTiles via `GPS_API.Maps`) whenever it is bigger than 80 px on screen at its nearest point (`MINIMAP_PX`), whatever its mesh level of detail; smaller ones use GPS's 64-px overview atlases. (Until 2026-10-14 the choice followed the mesh step, so a busy view with a coarse step showed large blurry patches.) Triangle textures come from a pool; tile meshes are cached for 3 s.
  - Buildings for the whole game: `Data/Detail_*.lua` (15 maps, 863 tiles with WMOs, 11,384 blocks, 52 MB in 21 files; generated by `scripts/browser_export_detail.js` + `scripts/build_detail.py`). Per tile with buildings, 66.7-yard blocks (8x8 per tile) of a 1-yard height raster (ground, water and every WMO face from above, steep roofs and spires included; highest wins) turned into a mesh by greedy Delaunay refinement (1 yd error near buildings, 4 yd elsewhere), at three levels: fine (`ns.Detail`, every sample), coarse (`ns.DetailLow`, every 4th sample) and far (`ns.DetailFar`, every 8th); the coarser levels take the sample as it is (no max filter, so they stay aligned with the minimap). Only real walls (WMO faces steeper than about 78 degrees) carry a colour, from their texture average; a steep mesh triangle is drawn flat-coloured only when its highest corner is on such a wall, everything else (roofs, spires, ground) shows the minimap. Blocks within 1,000 yd of the view centre are drawn on top of the grid while the camera is closer than 2,500 yd, nearest the centre first, until the budget (4,000 triangles, `/gps3d buildings <n>`, `/gps3d detail on|off`) is used. A block's level is the coarsest whose vertex spacing stays under 10 px on screen (`LEVEL_PX`); a block that does not fit the budget at that level is left flat rather than drawn coarser (that is what made dense quarters such as Stormwind's Mage Quarter look broken up). The grid stays drawn under them (its triangles there are sorted as if 30 yd further away), so seams never show the sky. Blocks are decoded when first needed and at most 1,500 triangles of new blocks are made into textures per frame. Cost measured in the sandbox (native Lua): about 2-4x the grid-only frame in cities at the default budget; +52 MB Lua memory and about 0.2 s parse at login.
  - Coordinates: world yards, x = gx * 533.33 (east), y = -gy * 533.33 (north) on the ADT grid; the view follows you between maps and says so on maps without terrain.
  - Back-face culling and 512 depth buckets (8 frames x 64 layer buckets).
  - You: the minimap arrow as a ground decal (a square on the terrain, bent into perspective with `SetVertexOffset` like the terrain, drawn above it) plus your character model (`SetModelByUnit("player")` in a ModelScene using the same camera). Height is `UnitPosition`'s Z when it is within 40 yd of the map surface, else the surface. `/gps3d marker arrow|model|both`, `/gps3d modelyaw <deg>`, `/gps3d pretend` (a stand-in walks round Northshire Abbey).
  - Camera: orbit point + distance + yaw + pitch (tilt 45 degrees above the ground, that is at most 45 from straight down, to straight down (`MIN_PITCH`, 30 until 2026-10-17); straight down, locked, is the default: the ring under the compass, or `/gps3d top`, turns the lock off and on; that ring shows a little 2x2 grid of ground the way the camera sees it, turned with the yaw like the compass needle and in perspective at the current tilt (square when straight down), four `Media/Cell.tga` squares bent with `SetVertexOffset` by `UpdateGrid`, gold while locked, and the choice is remembered in `GPS3D_DB.topDown`). Rings in the 3D window, top to bottom: compass (the needle turned by `SetRotation(rad(yaw))`), straight down / tilted, `3D` (back to 2D), camera mode, Me. Modes Free / Follow / Behind (the camera ring steps Follow -> Behind -> Free -> Follow; Me locks back on in the last lock, which is remembered; the footer ends with the mode): left-drag grabs the ground (switches to Free), right-drag turns and tilts, wheel zooms toward the cursor (Free) or around you, double-click flies there, compass turns north up. Behind keeps yaw = -facing + a right-drag offset, eased. Height (since 2026-10-15): panning keeps the camera's altitude (the orbit height `cam.th` stays; a drag, double-click or mouse-up no longer resets it to the ground under the view), so a mountain comes closer instead of lifting the camera. Only where the ground or a roof comes within `CLEARANCE` = 8 yd of the camera is it lifted (`Lift`, not stored: it drops back once you are past), and zooming in stops at that height (`Zoom` bisects the step). Since 2026-10-16 that clearance is checked over everything within 25 yd (`GroundUnder`: the camera's spot and 4 rings, 49 points), and, tilted, the view line must also clear the ground for `AHEAD` (1.2) x cos(tilt)^2 of the way to the point looked at (`LiftFor`, sampled every 6 yd), so a hill in front lifts the camera over it instead of filling the view (fades out toward straight down, where only the 8 yd count). Both are skipped when the camera is clearly above the highest point of the tiles involved (`TileTop`: grid maximum and the highest building vertex, read from the block strings once per tile). Triangles crossing the near plane are cut 1.5 yd in front of the camera and their front part drawn with 3 or 4 corners and adjusted texture coordinates (`DrawClipped`), instead of being hidden; cut parts wholly off screen are skipped. Before this, a low tilted camera looking into a slope showed sky through the ground. In Free mode a zoom keeps the height it ends at as your altitude. Grab and zoom use the real ground point under the cursor (`ScreenToTerrain`, a ray march), so it stays under the cursor whatever the altitude. Follow and Behind still orbit you at your height.
  - `/gps3d test` is its self-test.
  - Per-frame savings (see `PERFORMANCE.md`): a vertex that moved less than `VO_EPS` (0.25 px) keeps its last screen position (no `SetVertexOffset`); in Free mode, when only you move, only the marker is redrawn; no tables or closures are created per frame; only the drawn building level is decoded. `PERFORMANCE.md` also lists the measured but not yet applied one-texture-per-grid-cell change (`tools/sandbox/perf/quads.diff`) and other ideas.
- `scripts/browser_export.js`: runs in the MapExplorer page to read the game files and download `gps-export.zip`.
- `scripts/build_maptiles.py`: turns that zip into `Data/MapTiles.lua` and `Overview/` (add `--tga` if the client will not load JPEG), and saves `data/northshire_tin.json`.
- `scripts/build_tin.py`: the old single-tile Northshire mesh (`data/northshire_tin.json`); superseded by the building export below and no longer in the TOC.
- `scripts/browser_export_detail.js` (`gpsDetail.start({maps:[0]})`, then `gpsDetail.download()`, in MapExplorer after `browser_export.js`) and `scripts/build_detail.py`: the buildings, `data/detail_*.json` -> `prototypes/GPS3D/Data/Detail_*.lua` (files of at most 6 MB; every block decoded and checked). Add new files to `GPS3D.toc`.
- `scripts/browser_export_world.js` (`await gpsExportWorld()` in MapExplorer) and `scripts/build_world.py`: the whole-world terrain, `data/world_heights.json` -> `prototypes/GPS3D/Data/World_*.lua`. Add new files to `GPS3D.toc`.
- `scripts/export_settings.json`: settings for the coming 3D export (shape error 1 yard, distance levels, per-model overrides).
- `data/export.json`, `data/northshire_tin.json`: the raw export, for reference.
- `tools/sandbox/`: a browser sandbox that runs the addon files unchanged on an imitation of the WoW UI engine (Lua 5.4 in WebAssembly), with a simulated player; published privately as the "GPS Sandbox" artifact. See its README for rebuilding and the headless test scenarios.
- `TESTING.md`: the step-by-step in-game test, with expected self-test output and fixes for likely failures.
- `legacy/`: the first version (pre-cut 600 MB TGA tiles, `map_processor.py`, old `Core.lua`, `MapData.lua`); not used.

## Coordinates

The 64x64 ADT grid: tile (x, y) covers [x, x+1] x [y, y+1], x east, y south, 533 1/3 yards per tile. World X (north) and Y (west) map to it as `gx = 32 - Y / 533.33`, `gy = 32 - X / 533.33`. `UnitPosition("player")` returns X first, then Y, then Z and the map (instance) ID, which is the key into `ns.Maps`.

## Regenerating the data

1. Open https://coldensjo.github.io/ClassicWowMapExplorer/, choose `C:\Program Files (x86)\World of Warcraft`, game version Forever.
2. Paste `scripts/browser_export.js` into the page's console, move the camera once, then run `await gpsExport()`; it downloads `gps-export.zip`.
3. `python scripts/build_maptiles.py path/to/gps-export.zip`.

## Deploying and testing

Copy `Interface/AddOns/GnomishPositioningSystem` (and `prototypes/GPS3D` for the 3D view) to `C:\Program Files (x86)\World of Warcraft\_classic_beta_\Interface\AddOns`, then `/reload` in game. The TOC interface number is Forever's beta number (16001); update it when Forever goes live if the client reports the addon as out of date.

Nothing here has run in the game yet. The code was checked outside the game against a stubbed WoW API (lupa) and the real exported data: both addons loaded together, 53 checks of the shared look, the 2D/3D switch, the marker and the camera controls (since then `tools/sandbox/test_world.py`, 52 checks of the 3D view, and `test_look.py`, 27 checks of the "minimap rings" look, both passing on 2026-10-16); the marker's screen corners were also cross-checked against an independent renderer of the real game files in MapExplorer (identical to the pixel). Follow `TESTING.md`; results go in a `testing/` folder in this repository.

## API compatibility (checked 2026-10-13)

Every function, widget method, template and font the two addons use was checked against Forever's own UI source (Blizzard's `forever` branch, build 1.60.1.70338, via Gethe/wow-ui-source): all exist. Secret-value rules (Forever carries Midnight's addon restrictions): none of the values read (UnitPosition, GetPlayerFacing, C_Map positions, our own frames' size, position and scale) are marked as secret returns for frames like ours; the code still checks `issecretvalue` and treats hidden values as "position not available". The 8-argument `SetTexCoord` used for 3D triangles is the form Blizzard's Forever code uses (corners in the order upper-left, lower-left, upper-right, lower-right). `Bindings.xml` uses the `category="BINDING_HEADER_..."` form Blizzard's Forever bindings use. The look and the 3D marker added (checked against the same build): `BackdropTemplate` with `SetBackdrop`/`SetBackdropColor`/`SetBackdropBorderColor`, `CreateColor`, `Texture:SetGradient(orientation, minColor, maxColor)`, `FontString:SetWordWrap`/`GetStringWidth`, the fonts `GameFontNormalLarge`, `GameFontHighlight`, `GameFontHighlightSmall`, `GameFontNormalSmall`, `GameTooltip_Hide`, and the ModelScene actor's `SetModelByUnit` (marked `RequiresDeclassifiedUnitIdentity`; fine for "player" as far as the source says, the self-test reports it). `UnitPosition` documents a real `positionZ` return, used for your height. Not answerable from the source, so covered by the self-tests: whether addons may load minimap images by FileDataID, and whether JPEG loads from an addon folder.

### The "minimap rings" look (checked 2026-10-16, build 1.60.1.70425)

Forever runs the mainline client with its own "Camelot" skin (`[Game]` files for `camelot` in the TOCs), so the mainline art names below draw Forever's version of that art. Each is used by Forever's own interface: `Button:SetNormalAtlas`/`SetPushedAtlas`/`SetHighlightAtlas(atlas, blendMode)`, `Texture:SetAtlas`, `Texture:SetRotation`, `Texture:SetBlendMode`, `FontString:SetShadowOffset`/`SetShadowColor`, `Button:RegisterForClicks`, `GameTooltip:AddLine`/`IsOwned`; atlases `RedButton-Exit`, `RedButton-exit-pressed`, `RedButton-Highlight` (`UIPanelCloseButtonNoScripts`); files `Interface\Minimap\MiniMap-TrackingBorder`, `UI-Minimap-Background`, `UI-Minimap-ZoomButton-Highlight`, `MinimapArrow`. The art itself is not in Blizzard's source, so the sandbox shows stand-ins (`tools/sandbox/make_standins.py`); `TESTING.md` section 1 asks for a look in the game.

## Tech

Lua 5.1 (WoW), private namespace `local addonName, ns = ...`, no globals except `GPS_MapFrame`, `GPS_Toggle`, `GPS_API`, the slash command and the binding strings (GPS3D: `GPS3D_Frame`, `GPS3D_API`, `GPS3D_DB`, `/gps3d`). Python 3 + Pillow for the converter. `GPS3D.lua`'s main chunk is close to Lua 5.1's limit of 200 local variables (197 on 2026-10-17; the sandbox's Lua 5.4 does not catch it): put new helpers in `do` blocks or on tables, and check with `luac5.1 -p`.
