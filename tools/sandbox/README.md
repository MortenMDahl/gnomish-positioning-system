# GPS sandbox

A browser page that runs the addon files unchanged in a Lua VM (wasmoon, Lua 5.4 in WebAssembly;
Fengari as a fallback where WebAssembly is blocked), on a small imitation of the WoW UI engine.
Published privately as the "GPS Sandbox" artifact.

- `site/engine.lua`: the WoW UI engine (frames, anchors, layers, input, timers, slash commands,
  SavedVariables, GameTooltip, ModelScene with stand-in models). It returns a draw list per frame.
- `index.src.html`: the page (WebGL renderer, simulated player, chat). `python build.py` inlines the
  patched Fengari and writes `site/index.html`.
- `site/addons/` is filled at publish time with copies of `Interface/AddOns/GnomishPositioningSystem`
  (Core.lua, MapWindow.lua, Data/MapTiles.lua) and `prototypes/GPS3D` (GPS3D.lua, Models.lua and every file in its Data folder, as listed in GPS3D.toc);
  `site/overview/` with the addon's Overview images.
- `site/minimap/`: 25 real minimap tiles (Eastern Kingdoms 30..34 x 46..50) from the game files, via MapExplorer.
- `test_engine.py` (opens both windows and renders them to PNG; no checks), `test_world.py`, `test_look.py`: run engine + addons in Lua 5.4 (lupa) without a browser; `test_world.py` checks the 3D view across the world, buildings included (62 checks, the open sea drawn in the background's colour and the overview images' sea in one tone among them, caves and mines keeping their outside rock and the fine ground around them among them, heights read from the strings and one position rule with the 2D map among them); `test_look.py` checks the window look, "minimap rings" (34 checks: what each window shows, the rings and what they do, every ring's contents on the gold ring's centre (from `site/ui/minimap-trackingborder.png` itself), the straight-down ring's grid centred at every tilt and turn, the 3D view's `<` `>`, tooltips, layout at the smallest window size); `test_terrain.py` checks the ground's level of detail (5 checks: zooming 2 % a frame from 14,000 to 30 yd at Mount Hyjal, Durotar and Elwynn Forest, tilted and straight down, how far the drawn ground moves at fixed points from frame to frame; no cracks while morphing; five far views' height error on screen against the full grid; every tile coarsest just short of the planet's hand-over; every tile's levels in `Data/Lod_*.lua` or flat; `GPS3D_LUA=<file>` measures another `GPS3D.lua`, `TERRAIN_LUA=<file>` another `Terrain.lua`, `TERRAIN_QUICK=1` / `TERRAIN_PLACE=<name>` / `TERRAIN_NOZOOM=1` run less, `LOD_P=Q=0.8,TEX=0.03` tries the `Lod` parameters, `FAR_BUDGET=a,b,c,d,e` caps the far views' triangles, `TERRAIN_DUMP=<file>` writes the far views for `scripts/terrain_lod_study.py`); `perf_terrain.py [runs] label=GPS3D.lua ...` compares the ground's frame times of several `GPS3D.lua` at the far views and over the zoom sweeps (fresh processes in turn, the fastest of each kept; `shots/perf_terrain.json`); `scn_j8hyjal.py` (`TAG=before|after`) shoots Mount Hyjal from 5,000 yd tilted, plain and with each ground triangle shaded; `check_decode.py` compares the Lua and Python decoders of every building block; `perf_buildings.py` measures frame times with and without buildings.
- `run_page.py <scenario> [w h]`: serves `site/` and drives it in headless Chromium (`scn_*.py`; `DSF=2` for sharper screenshots; `scn_look.py` takes close-ups of both windows). It needs `site/wasmoon/index.js` and `fengari-web.js` (wasmoon 1.16.0 and fengari-web 0.1.4 from npm, not kept in the repository).
- `run_page.py <scenario> [w h]`: serves `site/` (on port 8765, or `SANDBOX_PORT`, when several sandboxes run at once) and drives it in headless Chromium (`scn_*.py`; `DSF=2` for sharper screenshots; `scn_look.py` takes close-ups of both windows). It needs `site/wasmoon/index.js` and `fengari-web.js` (wasmoon 1.16.0 and fengari-web 0.1.4 from npm, not kept in the repository).
- `test_camheight.py` (since 2026-10-21): the 3D camera's height over Orgrimmar, Stormwind and Thunder Bluff, zooming in (2 % a frame at 30 fps, straight down and tilted 45, Free and Follow) and panning slowly at a low height: the largest per-frame jump of the height and of the view not explained by the zoom or pan, never under a roof, the ease's time at 20, 30 and 60 fps, zooming still stopping at the clearance (9 checks; `--file X.lua` runs another GPS3D.lua for before/after, `--fast` draws fewer triangles, `--only zoom|pan|ease`).
  `CSP="..." python run_page.py csp` checks the fallback under a strict content security policy.

- `site/minimap/`: 131 of the game's minimap images (Elwynn, Stormwind, Westfall, Redridge, Duskwood; exported from MapExplorer with `minimapThumbnails`, JPEG 85 %); other tiles show their part of the overview image, so they look blurrier than in the game.
- `make_standins.py`: draws stand-ins in `site/ui/` for game art the sandbox has no copy of (the dark disc behind the rings, the hover glow, the red close-button atlases). They only approximate the game's art: judge layout in the sandbox, the art in the game. GPS's own icons come from `scripts/make_media.py`.
- Engine: `Texture:SetAtlas` and the button atlas setters draw `atlas:<name>` (mapped to images in the page's `UI_FILES`); strings sent to the page now escape backslashes, so texture paths such as `Media\Needle` arrive intact.
- Traditional map (GPS's third mode, `MapWindow.lua`): the engine has a stand-in for Blizzard's map canvas (`MapCanvasMixin`, the `MapCanvasFrameScrollContainerTemplate` scroll frame, the data provider mixins) and for a few of the game's uiMaps (`S.uiMaps`: Azeroth, the two continents, Stormwind, Westfall, Elwynn, Dun Morogh, Durotar, and The Deadmines with two floors), with `C_Map` positions worked out from their world boxes. It draws a parchment sheet with the child maps as boxes (click one to open it, right-click for the map above, wheel zoom, drag to pan when zoomed) and your arrow; Blizzard's art and pins are only in the game. "Inside a dungeon" in the place list puts you in The Deadmines (no world position, the dungeon's map and a spot on it), and **Go** fires `PLAYER_ENTERING_WORLD` and `ZONE_CHANGED_NEW_AREA` like a loading screen.
- `test_2d.py`: the 2D map's own logic (15 checks: zoom keeps the point under the cursor, live minimap tiles from `LIVE_MIN_SCALE`, the arrow on your position, a map fits its main land, following you onto another continent and every frame; both continents side by side, with five mutations).
- `test_wmo_groups.js` (`node tools/sandbox/test_wmo_groups.js`): `scripts/browser_wmo_groups.js` on made-up WMO models (8 checks: group ranges from the stored boxes, boxes much wider than their vertices, a tunnel inside its shell's box, the baked light deciding where the boxes do not).
- `test_blocks.py` (8 checks, since 2026-10-21): the building blocks close up at Orgrimmar's Valley of Strength and Stormwind's Trade District, 40 to 300 yd, straight down and tilted 45: share of the blocks in view drawn raised, triangles, levels by distance, frame times; zooming in 2 % a frame from 600 yd (blocks appearing, going flat or changing level per frame; triangles made per frame); the Mage Quarter's coarse walls. `MEASURE=1` prints only the measurements, `J2_GPS3D=<file>` loads another `GPS3D.lua` (to measure the code before a change in turn with the new). `scn_j2views.py` takes the Orgrimmar views as shots (80 and 150 yd, tilted).
- `test_luac.py`: every shipped Lua file, data included, compiled with `luac5.1` (the game's Lua; the sandbox's 5.4 does not enforce its limits), and how close each code file is to 200 locals in the main chunk and 60 upvalues in a function (3 checks).
- `test_modes.py`: the three modes on the stand-in (63 checks: `<` `>` saving that you stopped following, `/gps grid` from the 3D view, the 3D window keeping its place and size and `/gps3d` opening through the modes among them: the mode ring both ways, which mode opens, the switch on entering and leaving a dungeon, floors, Me, zoom, focus between modes, saved size, slash commands, self-test lines). `scn_modes.py` does the same in the page and saves `shots/modes_*.png`.
- `test_blizzard_canvas.py`: runs `MapWindow.lua` on Blizzard's real map-canvas code (Blizzard_MapCanvas and the SharedXMLBase pieces it needs) from a clone of Gethe/wow-ui-source, branch `forever`: `WOWUI=/path/to/clone python test_blizzard_canvas.py` (15 checks). The templates are built as their XML describes and the game's art calls are stubbed; it checks, for example, that the `BorderFrame` child `MapCanvasMixin.OnLoad` reaches into is there.
- Settings: the engine keeps what addons register in the game's Settings panel (`Settings.RegisterVerticalLayoutCategory`, `RegisterProxySetting`, checkboxes, sliders, dropdowns, section headers, buttons, `RegisterAddOnCategory`, `OpenToCategory`), with the argument checks of Forever's `Blizzard_Settings_Shared`; the page's **Options** button (or `/gps options`) draws it as Options > AddOns, and every control changes the real setting (`SANDBOX_SETTINGS`, `SANDBOX_SETTING_SET`, `SANDBOX_SETTING_CLICK`). `test_settings.py` checks GPS's page (46 checks: the sections and their order, each default against the code's own starting value, each control's effect on the map, the dungeon setting, `/gps options`, GPS without GPS3D and without the Settings API); `scn_settings.py` does it in the page and saves `shots/settings_*.png`.
- Quests: the engine stands in for the quest log (7 made-up quests in Elwynn Forest and Westfall, each with a point and, most, an objective area as an ellipse in its zone's coordinates, quest 33's as two joined ellipses (a waisted shape with a concave edge); `S.quests`), `C_SuperTrack` (quest 33 tracked at start), `C_QuestLog.GetQuestsOnMap` / `GetQuestObjectives` / `GetTitleForQuestID`, `C_Map.GetMapChildrenInfo` and map types, `GetCVarBool("questPOI")`, Blizzard's quest button (`POIButtonTemplate`: a gold disc with the quest's number, `?` when complete; click tracks) and the game's quest-area frame (`QuestPOIFrame`: draws the asked quests' ellipses, answers the hit-test; `S.blobAnswers` makes the hit-test answer `"always"` (default), only while the frame is `"shown"`, only while it is `"drawn"` at an alpha above 0, or `"never"`, to try GPS's fallbacks; `S.blobHits` counts the calls). The traditional map's stand-in draws the same buttons and the tracked quest's area (stand-ins for `QuestDataProviderMixin` and `QuestBlobDataProviderMixin`). The art (`ui/questpoi.png`, `ui/questblob.png`) is from `make_standins.py`. `test_quests.py` checks GPS's quests (37 checks: 2D markers at their world positions worked out independently from the stand-in maps' boxes, through pan and zoom; the tracked one selected; click to track; tooltip; the setting and `questPOI`; another map; the 2D area frame over its zone's rectangle; hover; the 3D markers at their projected ground points through a zoom, pan, turn and tilt; the 3D objective areas (GPS3D's `QuestAreas.lua`): the shape against the stand-in's, on the ground, layer order, hover, the setting, `questPOI`, the planet, the ways round the hit-test, the game without it, with sampling and frame costs printed; the `quests:` lines of `/gps test`). `scn_quests.py` does it in the page (`shots/quests_*.png`; `quests_3d_area.png` and `quests_3d_area_tilted.png` show quest 33's area on the ground).
- Zones: `site/zones.lua` (written by `make_zones.py` from MapExplorer's `regions.json`: the area of every 33-yard chunk of the Eastern Kingdoms and Kalimdor from the game's map files, area names from the VMaNGOS database) gives the engine all 53 zones (51 from the chunks, Ironforge and Undercity from the classic world map's rectangles). Each is a stand-in uiMap (the game's classic uiMap ID where known, else 100000 + its area ID) whose rectangle is the box round its chunks; `C_Map.GetBestMapForUnit` / `GetMapPosFromWorldPos` answer from the chunk under the point, and the zone texts (`GetZoneText`, `GetSubZoneText`, the page's readout) follow your position instead of the last place you went to. The traditional map's sheet shows every zone (faint boxes, which overlap; a click opens the zone under the cursor). Loaded after `engine.lua` by the page and every test. `test_modes.py` checks the title on every one of the 53 zones and the zone texts there; `scn_zones.py` shows it in the page (`shots/zones_*.png`).
- Debug log: the engine has `seterrorhandler` / `geterrorhandler` (Lua errors go through the handler, so GPS's hook sees them), `debugstack`, the TOC fields (`SANDBOX_ADDONMETA`, which the page reads from the `.toc` files) and an `EditBox` (its first 60 lines drawn) for the copy window. The page's **Debug log** button shows GPS's log as the game would save it (turn on, clear, copy all), and the page keeps it across Reload like the game keeps saved variables. `test_debug.py` (28 checks) and `scn_debug.py` (`shots/debug_panel.png`).
- Other addons (GPS's `Compat.lua`): the engine has `hooksecurefunc` (post-hook, the original's results returned), `S.xmlTemplates[name] = function(frame)` for other addons' XML templates a test defines, and separate mouse click / motion switches (one switch underneath). `fixtures/gathermate2_standin.lua` is a stand-in of GatherMate2 written for the tests (its public functions, its map layer and pin mixin, with the same names and behaviour as GatherMate2's, checked against its source); no GatherMate2 code. `test_compat_canvas.py` (24 checks on Blizzard's real map canvas: layers copied, Blizzard's untouched, pin libraries and broken layers handled, updates, removal, the setting; five mutations; a run without `issecurevariable`) and `test_compat.py` (19 checks: GatherMate2's nodes on the 2D map and in the 3D view, the shared marker pools hiding only what was shown; `GATHERMATE_SRC=<GatherMate2 checkout>` adds the check that GPS holds none of its code). The page does not load the stand-in.
- The game's models (GPS3D's `Models.lua`): the ModelScene stand-in draws each file model as boxes of its real size and turn (`GPS3D_API.ModelInfo`: radius, bottom, top; yaw, pitch and roll applied as the addon sets them): tan for buildings (kept to 40 yd, since some of the game's buildings reach far past their walls), a trunk and a green crown for tall models, a grey box for the rest. `Actor:GetActiveBoundingBox` returns the model's box once loaded. `S.modelLoadDelay` (seconds, 0 by default) delays loading; `SANDBOX_WMO = false` makes buildings never load (the game refusing them in an addon's ModelScene; the page starts that way and runs the check again at every load, since it can only draw boxes for buildings); the page's "game refuses WMOs" and "game loads WMOs" buttons switch it and run `/gps3d models wmo`. `S.modelLoads` counts `SetModelByFileID` calls. `test_models.py` (50 checks, caves and mines among them, props and trees off by default, Nordrassil drawn once, buildings the game does not draw keeping their shapes under Stormwind's footprint; ten mutations), `perf_models.py` (Lua-side cost with props off, 250 and 1000), `scn_models.py`, `scn_models_top.py` and `scn_models_sw.py` (Stormwind as the page opens) (shots: tilted over Goldshire, the game refusing WMOs, props off; top-down with and without props, to compare with the minimap image).
- The planet view (GPS3D's `Globe.lua`): the page lists `Data/Globe.lua` and `Globe.lua` with the other GPS3D files and maps `Interface\AddOns\GPS3D\Globe\*` to `site/globe/*.png` (written by `scripts/build_globe.py`). `test_globe.py` checks it in Lua (55 checks, the last that twenty-two mutations are each caught: the clouds and the Maelstrom's storm over the land and the sea (draw order, and every point of their images at least half its height above the sea, at views like the reported one and while spinning), spinning round and round through the far side, the planet staying upright, the compass's north, flights from the far side, the stop short of the poles, the clouds and the Maelstrom's storm and lightning, the layout from a stand-in world map and the fallback, the sphere's maths, the view straightening, the close-up view unchanged to the pixel, the hand-over to the pixel, the planet, sky and arrow, picking, the controls, spinning to the other continent, flying down, Me, `<` `>`, zooming in over the sea, other maps, following, the self-test, frame times, crossings, memory). `check_globezoom.py` checks it in the page: `scn_globezoom.py` zooms out from 12,000 to 80,000 yd in steps of 4 % at three window sizes (`GZ_W`, `GZ_H`) and tilted 50 degrees (`GZ_TILT`) and saves each frame (8 checks); each is compared with the one before shrunk by the step, and no step may jump (more than 1.8 x its larger neighbour) and the hand-over step may be no bigger than 1.6 x the steps before it. `scn_globe.py` (`GLOBE_DISTS`) and `scn_globeviews.py` take screenshots (`shots/globe_*.png`, `shots/gv_*.png`; `GV_W`, `GV_H` the window's size, `GV_VIEWS` some views only, `GV_TAG` a prefix for the names); run a scenario by its name without `scn_` (`python3 run_page.py globeviews`). Known sandbox quirk: a quest marker partly faded (between 14,000 and 18,000 yd) can vanish in the page although the engine sends it with the right alpha; in the game a frame's alpha fades its children as usual.
- The 2D map with both continents (GPS's `World.lua`; the page lists it after `Core.lua`): `test_2d.py` checks it (15 checks, the last that five mutations are each caught); `scn_world2d.py` takes screenshots zoomed out and over the sea between them (`shots/w2d_*.png`). Known sandbox quirk, also on main: zoomed far out on the 2D map, the page draws the map's name in huge stretched letters along the bottom of the window; GPS makes no such text (it comes from one of the page's stand-ins), so the game will not show it.
- `scn_sea.py`: the 2D map and the 3D view zoomed far out over the west coast of the Eastern Kingdoms, to check that the sea past a map's last tiles has the same colour as the open sea in the minimap images (`shots/sea_*.png`).
- `scn_seaview.py` and `measure_sea.py` (2026-10-21): the open sea in the 3D view and its hand-over to the planet at the views of Morten's reports (Kalimdor's north from 9,000 and 12,000 yd straight down and tilted; NE of Azshara from 6,000 to 30,000 yd), `SV_TAG=<tag> python3 run_page.py seaview 1400 900` (`SV_NOCLOUDS=1` hides the planet's sky, `SV_2D=1` adds the 2D map), then `python3 measure_sea.py <tag>`: per view the spread of the open sea's colours, the share of the minimap's darker sea tone, the colours on either side of the ground's edge in the sea and along the old tan line NE of Azshara. The page's blending keeps the canvas opaque where something half transparent is drawn over an opaque picture (`blendFuncSeparate`, since 2026-10-21; before, the page showed through there: lighter rectangles under the planet's half-faded overview images at 19,000 yd that the game does not have).
- Map key and full screen (GPS's `MapKey.lua`, `Core.lua`'s full screen; since 2026-10-21): the engine stands in for key bindings (`S.bindings`: Blizzard's map key on M, the quest log on L; `GetBindingKey`, `GetBindingAction(key, checkOverride)`, `SetOverrideBinding`, `SetOverrideBindingClick`, `ClearOverrideBindings`, each change answered with UPDATE_BINDINGS; `SANDBOX_BIND(command, keys...)` rebinds, `SANDBOX_PRESS(key)` runs the override or the binding, `SANDBOX_BINDINGS_XML(xml)` reads an addon's Bindings.xml) and for combat (`SANDBOX_COMBAT(on)`: `InCombatLockdown`, PLAYER_REGEN_DISABLED / ENABLED; binding changes in combat are refused and listed in `S.blocked`). `test_mapkey.py` (35 checks, the last that 22 mutations are each caught by their own check) adds a stand-in of Blizzard's `WorldMapFrame` (its BorderFrame, CloseButton and MaximizeMinimizeFrame as `Blizzard_WorldMap.xml` places them, `ShowUIPanel` / `HideUIPanel` refusing addons in combat, `ToggleWorldMap`). `scn_mapkey.py` takes the top right of the 2D and 3D windows, the globe's tooltip and both windows at full screen (`shots/mapkey_*.png`); `make_standins.py` draws stand-ins of the maximize / minimize atlases (`RedButton-Expand`, `RedButton-Condense`).
